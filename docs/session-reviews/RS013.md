## Round-3 MAJOR (recon race): closed — verified

`reconcile_state` now exempts rows younger than `grace_s`
(`src/core/state_manager.py:169-190`). I drove the real `StateManager`
(`":memory:"`) through the real `_perform_reconciliation` across the whole
behaviour matrix, including the ways a grace window is normally gamed:

```
A fresh row (age 0s), uncorroborated:                pendings=[111] telegrams=0
B aged 300s, uncorroborated (external delete):       pendings=[]    telegrams=1
C age 119s, uncorroborated:                          pendings=[111] telegrams=0
C age 121s, uncorroborated:                          pendings=[]    telegrams=1
D time_placed=0, uncorroborated:                     pendings=[]    telegrams=1
D time_placed=NULL, uncorroborated:                  pendings=[]    telegrams=1
E repeated ticks then aged past grace:               pendings=[]    telegrams=1
F time_placed 1h in the FUTURE (backward clock jump):pendings=[111] telegrams=0
G aged 300s but corroborated by heartbeat orders:    pendings=[111] telegrams=0
```

- The race is closed (A) and ghost semantics survive (B, C-121, E) — a row
  cannot be kept alive by repeated ticks; only age matters.
- `(r['time_placed'] or 0) < cutoff` gives NULL/0 rows **no** exemption (D) —
  the safe direction; a pre-`time_placed` row still sweeps immediately.
- Backward clock jump (F) extends the exemption by the jump size; direction is
  keep-the-row / over-count, i.e. conservative. A forward jump only shortens
  grace back toward the round-3 behaviour. Bounded, ungraded.

Interaction check, all clear: `_cleanup_ghost_orders` TTL is 7200 s ≫ 120 s

the CLOSED/archive path is by ticket and never routes through
`reconcile_state`; `sync_positions` is driven from heartbeat positions, not DB
